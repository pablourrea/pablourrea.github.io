DrupalCamp 2024: Benidorm

Drupal
Events

DrupalCamp 2024 banner - Spanish Drupal Association (Scaled)

Pablo Urrea

10 minutes reading

I’ve been absent here for a while, and for good reason. I was enjoying a trip to the favorite Spanish city of grandparents, British visitors, and this year, attendees of the DrupalCamp 2024, the annual regional event that brings together Drupal developers in Spain.

 

Thursday, October 24, Business Day. The event kicked off on Thursday, and as usual, the first day was dedicated to networking between companies and developers. While my adventure didn’t start until the next day, it’s true that this day is quite useful for getting to know companies offering solutions in the Drupal ecosystem, whether as a potential client or as a possible employee if you’re job hunting.

 

Friday, October 25, first day. My alarm went off at 6 a.m. A bus was waiting for me (but not for long) to take me to Benidorm. Despite the bus being comfortable and spacious, the nearly six-hour journey couldn’t be eased by even the world’s coziest seat. Once there, I stretched my back, which felt like a moving box, and made sure to settle the shortbread and juice given to me by the bus driver.

 

I searched for the bus stop, located across from the Benidorm station—a station that, by the way, is an abandoned shopping mall where no floors are accessible. Benidorm buses don’t accept card payments, something that seems obvious once you know it, but there I was, without a single coin, walking to the hotel with my suitcase and the roll-up banner of JAKALA. For someone coming from a big city, the thirty-minute walk was fairly pleasant, though I would have preferred to cut that time in half by bus.

Once in my hotel room on the 18th floor (21 if you count the restaurant, dance, and show floors), I finally got to do what I was most looking forward to: eating the Spanish omelet sandwich I had with me. I ate in a bit of a rush, as the first talk after lunch was about to begin. It was a pity I didn’t arrive earlier; I would have loved to appear in the group photo.

 

About half an hour before the first afternoon talk, I arrived at the hotel hosting the event: the Hotel Deloix Aqua Center 4****, a massive rectangular building with a central courtyard (where the group photo was taken). At reception, I was handed a name badge and a bag with goodies from the event sponsors, which this year included a full-sized towel, a waterproof phone bag, and a tourist guide to Benidorm.

At 3:00 p.m., I attended the first talk, “Theory and Practice of Drupal Recipes” by Manuel Adán Bonald, who explained one of Drupal’s new features: installation recipes. At 4:00 p.m., in the same room, Borja Sanz Sanz presented “From a Design System to Decoupled Portals,” where he showcased an ecosystem similar to what we use at JAKALA for building decoupled websites using Drupal as a backend and Next.js as a frontend.

Next came the “Coffee Break,” with an incredible selection of snacks and beverages. At 5:00 p.m., “Introduction to Caching in Drupal” by Luis Ruiz Peidro, a former colleague, highlighted the importance of properly implementing Drupal caching. Later, at 6:15 p.m., Alejandro Arnau presented “Content Structure and Cache Tags in Drupal: A Vital Relationship for Superior Performance,” with a more basic focus on cache usage.

To wrap up the day, I joined about twenty colleagues for dinner at an Indian restaurant near the hotel, where we enjoyed a delicious meal before calling it a night.

 

Saturday, October 26, second and final day. The day began on the battlefield: the hotel breakfast buffet. After braving the variety of food so early in the morning, I attended Saturday’s first talk at 10:00 a.m.: “How will Starshot Redefine Drupal for a New Generation of Users” by Lauri Timmanee. This talk exceeded my high expectations, showcasing how Starshot will provide a complete overhaul for Drupal.

 

At 11:00 a.m., Dennis A. Torres presented “Content FactorIA: Autonomous Content Generation in Drupal,” exploring content generation through artificial intelligence. At 12:00 p.m., Mauricio Dinarte explained the basics of DDEV for local Drupal environments in “Local Development Environments for Drupal with DDEV,” followed at 1:00 p.m. by João Bruges Galamba’s “AI-Assisted Module Development,” showcasing AI-driven development methods.

In the afternoon, at 3:00 p.m., I attended “API Security in Drupal: Tools, Best Practices, and Strategies for Securing APIs,” where Jose Manuel Ortega Candel shared valuable strategies for securing APIs. At 4:00 p.m., Óskar Calvo presented “Using Hux for Module Creation,” demonstrating how to implement event listeners instead of hooks in pre-Drupal 10 versions.

Saturday’s snack break didn’t surpass Friday’s, but it was still decent. Finally, Pedro González Serrano’s talk, “Supplying Drupal* Projects: Billing All Materials,” analyzed tools for managing the Drupal ecosystem on servers. The day ended with a closing talk, raffles, and the announcement of the venue for DrupalCamp 2025: Santiago de Compostela.

The closing dinner, held on the rooftop of the hotel’s third floor, was spectacular. The catering was excellent, though the cold reminded me that a jacket would have been a good idea.

 

In summary, the event was incredible. The speakers, talks, and organizing team demonstrated an impressive level of professionalism. Congratulations to all of them for making this year the leading Drupal event in Spain.

 

Thanks to JAKALA for taking me to DrupalCamp 2024 and sponsoring the event. And of course, thanks to all the Drupal enthusiasts I met. See you in Santiago de Compostela next year.
